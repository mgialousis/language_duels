## Lesson Details

### 📚 Lesson a2_g01: A Class Simple Past (ENHANCED)

**File:** `a2_g01_simple_past.json`

**Key Patterns:**

**Stem Changes (Sigmatic Aorist):**
- **-ν, -θ, -ζ → -σ** (πληρώνω → πλήρωσα, αρχίζω → άρχισα)
- **-κ, -γ, -χ, -χν, -σκ → -ξ** (ανοίγω → άνοιξα, διδάσκω → δίδαξα)
- **-π, -β, -φ, -πτ → -ψ** (γράφω → έγραψα, κόβω → έκοψα)
- **-αυ, -ευ → -αψ, -εψ** (δουλεύω → δούλεψα, παύω → έπαψα)

**Augment Rule:**
- Short verbs (2 syllables starting with consonant) add **ε-** prefix
- γράφω → **έ**γραψα (2 syl → needs augment)
- ανοίγω → άνοιξα (3 syl → no augment needed)

**Accent Rule:**
- Always on 3rd-from-last syllable (antepenultimate)

**Content:**
- 3 conjugation tables (γράφω, ανοίγω, πληρώνω)
- 4 examples with highlights
- 8 exercises including full conjugation

### 📚 Lesson a2_g06: B1/B2 Class Simple Past (NEW)

**File:** `a2_g06_simple_past_b_class.json`

**Key Pattern:**
Both B1 and B2 classes use the **same -ησα pattern** in aorist!

**Endings:**
- -ησα, -ησες, -ησε, -ήσαμε, -ήσατε, -ήσαν(ε)

**Formation:**
- B1: αγαπώ → αγάπ- + ησα = **αγάπησα**
- B2: θεωρώ → θεωρ- + ησα = **θεώρησα**

**Why Important:**
- Simpler than A class - no stem consonant changes!
- Just remove present ending and add -ησα

**Content:**
- 3 conjugation tables (μιλάω, αγαπάω, θεωρώ)
- 4 examples
- 7 exercises including full conjugation

**File:** `a2_g07_simple_past_irregular.json`

**Essential Irregular Verbs:**

| Present | Aorist | Meaning |
|---------|--------|---------|
| πάω | **πήγα** | I went |
| τρώω | **έφαγα** | I ate |
| πίνω | **ήπια** | I drank |
| βλέπω | **είδα** | I saw |
| λέω | **είπα** | I said |
| έρχομαι | **ήρθα** | I came |
| παίρνω | **πήρα** | I took |
| φεύγω | **έφυγα** | I left |
| βρίσκω | **βρήκα** | I found |

**Key Point:**
- Stems change completely and must be memorized
- Endings remain regular: -α, -ες, -ε, -αμε, -ατε, -αν

### 📚 Lesson a2_g08: Passive Simple Past (NEW)

**File:** `a2_g08_simple_past_passive.json`

**Key Pattern:**
ALL verb classes use the **same -ηκα endings** in passive aorist!

**Endings:**
- -ηκα, -ηκες, -ηκε, -ήκαμε, -ήκατε, -ήκαν(ε)

**Passive Stem Formation:**
- A class: Often adds -τ- or -θ- (γράφω → γράφ**τ**ηκα)
- B classes: Add -ηθ- (αγαπώ → αγαπ**ήθ**ηκα)

**Examples:**
| Active | Passive Aorist | Meaning |
|--------|----------------|---------|
| γράφω | γράφτηκα | I was written / I enrolled |
| αγαπώ | αγαπήθηκα | I was loved |
| θεωρώ | θεωρήθηκα | I was considered |

**Content:**
- 3 conjugation tables (γράφομαι, αγαπιέμαι, θεωρούμαι)
- 4 examples
- 6 exercises including full conjugation

## Key Grammar Findings from Research

### Simple Past (Αόριστος) Usage
> "The Aoristos is an ideal tense to describe an action that happens at a particular point in the past. It gives no information on how long it took, but is used for an action that happened just once in the past — so NOT an action that was repeated often." - Omilo

### Stem Change Patterns (A Class)
> "When you conjugate Verbs of Category A in 'aoristos', you need to change the last letter of the stem" - Omilo

### B Class Simplicity
> "When you conjugate Verbs of Category B1 in 'aoristos', you take the stem and you add: -ησα, -ησες, -ησε, -ήσαμε, -ήσατε, -ήσανε/-ήσαν. When you conjugate Verbs of Category B2, you follow the same rules." - Omilo

### Passive Uniformity
> "The passive aorist uses consistent markers: -ηκα, -ήκαμε, -ηκες, -ήκατε, -ηκε, -ήκαν across all conjugation types." - Cromwell International
